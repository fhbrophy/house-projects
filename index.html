Front yard re-do

Idea: desert/xeriscape front yard: decomposed granite (DG), desert plants on drip, maybe a paver path or entry, low-voltage lighting, and possibly a small turf patch.

All costs are rough 2026 Phoenix-area ranges from web cost guides, not quotes. Typical front yard assumed ~500–1,000 sq ft.

Difficulty: moderate overall. Rock, plants and drip are very DIY-able; grading, caliche digging, pavers and turf base are heavy work.

DIY vs pro: DIY the plants, drip, rock and lighting. Hire a pro (or rent equipment) for grass/sod removal, grading, and any pavers or turf, since a bad base sinks or drains poorly. Get 2–3 quotes.

Arizona heat, dog & HOA notes
  • Work season Oct–Apr. Avoid heavy outdoor work May–Sep; if you must, start at dawn and stop by late morning.
  • Plant timing: fall (Oct–Nov) is the best time to plant desert plants; early spring is second best. Cacti/succulents do fine in warm months.
  • Dog: pick non-toxic, non-spiny plants near paths; DG and rock get very hot, so leave shaded paths. Pet turf needs a drainage base and antimicrobial infill, and it gets very hot in full sun.
  • HOA: most Mesa HOAs need architectural approval for plants, rock color and hardscape. Get approval before buying anything.
  • Mesa rebate: the City of Mesa Grass-to-Xeriscape program pays about $1,000–2,100 if you remove 500+ sq ft of live grass. You need pre-approval before you start, a 2" mulch/rock layer, drip (no spray), and no black plastic. Check whether turf areas qualify.
  • Call 811 (Arizona 811) before digging.
Key materials
  • DG: 1/4" minus for paths (compacts), 1/2" screened for beds; earth tones hide dust
  • Woven/breathable landscape fabric or pre-emergent (not black plastic)
  • Desert plants: e.g. red yucca, desert spoon, Texas sage, lantana, brittlebush, agave (keep spiny ones away from the dog), a palo verde or mesquite for shade
  • Drip: 1/2" poly line, emitters, pressure regulator, filter, smart controller
  • Pavers + compacted base + sand; low-voltage LED fixtures + transformer; edging and boulders

Living room flooring: carpet to LVP

Goal: replace the worst of the living room carpet (worn by the dogs, plus puppy urine accidents) with LVP, picked for pet durability and waterproofing. It has to meet, and go with, the kitchen's large-format light beige/cream marble-look tile. Today there's a clean butt joint between light grayish-beige carpet and the tile.

Color direction: light wood-look in greige, soft beige, light oak or warm gray. Avoid dark woods. Aim to complement the tile, not match it exactly.

Specs: 20 mil+ wear layer (22+ is better for active dogs) · rigid SPC core · waterproof · textured/matte finish · ceramic or aluminum oxide coating.

Shortlist (Home Depot prices checked Nov 1 2026; they change, so check before buying)

  • Lifeproof Lighthouse Oak (22 mil): $3.29/sq ft ($66.12 per 20.1 sq ft case). Home Depot lists it in the gray color family.
  • Lifeproof Fresh Oak (22 mil): $3.28/sq ft ($65.99/case).
  • COREtec Ravenswood Oak (20 mil): COREtec Pro Premium; price through a dealer.
  • Lifeproof Vesinet Oak (22 mil): $3.28/sq ft on the product page, and as low as $2.98/sq ft in some listings. Beige color family.
  • Other light beige options at Home Depot to sample: Lifeproof Praline White Oak, Fairview Oak.

Dogs: LVP is not scratch-proof, and warranties usually exclude pet scratches. Textured finishes hide marks. Keep nails trimmed and cut down on grit with doormats and regular sweeping. It's far better than hardwood. Tile resists scratches better, but LVP is warmer and quieter.

Prep & transition
  • Treat urine odor first: after pulling the carpet, clean the slab with an enzyme cleaner, let it dry, then roll on an odor-blocking sealer.
  • Transition at the tile: keep the butt joint if the heights work; otherwise use a low-profile T-molding or reducer.
  • Samples: get physical samples and check them against the tile in real daylight and in the evening.
  • Hard, pet-friendly, waterproof flooring can help resale.
Arizona notes
  • Let the planks acclimate indoors with the AC running, about 48 hours. Lifeproof says it needs none, but it doesn't hurt.
  • Leave expansion gaps at walls; floors by sunny windows and patio doors get hot.
  • Check the product's direct-sun/heat rating; consider blinds or UV film on the hot windows.
  • Float it over the slab with a vapor barrier or an attached pad (Lifeproof has one; it allows 6-mil poly over concrete).
  • Never glue down over urine-damaged areas without sealing them first.

DIY vs pro: click-lock LVP is a reasonable DIY, moderate difficulty. The work is pulling carpet, pad, tack strip and staples, undercutting the door jambs, and doing the transitions. A pro runs about $4–9/sq ft installed, plus carpet removal.

Example only, a 300 sq ft living room: LVP ≈ $1,200–2,700 pro / $600–1,500 DIY (Lifeproof at about $3.28/sq ft is ≈ $1,050 in planks with 5–10% waste, before trim, sealer and tools).
